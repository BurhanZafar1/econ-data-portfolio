# Extending the crosswalk back to the first census (1951)

Third release, 5 November 2026. This note explains what exists for the censuses of 1951, 1961, 1972 and 1981, what the tables in this release contain, and what cannot be built from the published sources.

## 1. What PCO already did
The Population Census Organisation's *Population of Administrative Units 1951-98* (Census Publication 161) does not print the old censuses on their own administrative maps. It prints them on the 1998 frame: for every 1998 district, and for most 1998 tehsils, the population at each of the five censuses on the 1998 boundary. Data Darbar's parsing of the report (table census_admin_units_1951_1998) confirms this: "the units are the 1998 frame, with each earlier census restated onto it". Where a 1998 unit could not be carved out of an old count, the report prints a dash and the people stay inside the parent unit's figure.

So the heavy lifting for 1951 to 1981 is done by PCO, and the main thing this release adds is to carry those restated figures through the 1998 -> 2023 crosswalk, and to reconstruct the district maps of each census so that data published on the old districts (for example a 1981 district census report, or an election result by district) can be moved onto the 1998 and 2023 frames.

Coverage of the PCO restatement on the 424-unit 1998 frame used here (share of the 1998 population whose unit has a figure): 1951 90.1 percent (250 units), 1961 94.9 percent (349), 1972 97.5 percent (364), 1981 99.8 percent (418), 1998 100 percent. At district level every district has a figure from 1972 on; in 1961 Kohistan and Lower Dir are missing; in 1951 twenty units are missing (sixteen Balochistan districts that did not exist and were counted inside Kalat, Sibi, Quetta-Pishin, Loralai, Zhob or Chagai, plus Orakzai, Batagram, Kohistan and Lower Dir).

## 2. Files in this release
- **population_panel_1998_frame.csv** (424 rows) and **population_panel_1998_frame_districts.csv** (114 rows): population at 1951, 1961, 1972, 1981 and 1998 on the 1998 frame, straight from PCO, with the footnote mark and a flag for every cell saying whether PCO printed a figure.
- **population_panel_2023_frame.csv** (590 rows) and **population_panel_2023_frame_districts.csv** (135 rows): the same five censuses plus 2017 and 2023 on the Census 2023 units. 1951 to 1998 values are the 1998-frame figures pushed through the 1998 -> 2023 population weights; 2017 is PBS's own restatement on 2023 units; 2023 is the count. A flag per cell says "complete" or names the 1998 source units that had no PCO figure for that year. The 2017 and 2023 district totals reproduce PBS's district rows exactly.
- **district_lineage_1951_1998.csv** (128 rows): for every 1998 district (and for Hazara, Dir, Makran, Quetta-Pishin, Kachhi, Umerkot and the Karachi districts, which existed at some censuses but are not 1998 units), the year it was created, the district(s) it was carved from, former names, whether it existed at each census date, a one-sentence note, the source read, and a grade (86 verified, 35 reported, 7 unverified; the unverified rows are the six Frontier Regions, whose creation dates were not found, and the British-era Bolan Pass district).
- **district_frames_by_census.csv**: the district map at each census as reconstructed from the lineage (57 districts in 1951, 58 in 1961, 59 in 1972, 73 in 1981, 114 in 1998), each with its 1998-frame members, its population, and whether every member has a PCO figure.
- **crosswalk_district_historical_to_1998.csv** (479 rows) and **crosswalk_district_historical_to_2023.csv** (604 rows): the crosswalk from each census's district map to the 1998 districts, and chained to the 2023 districts. Weight = PCO's restated population of the member on the 1998 boundary divided by the historical district's total, for that census year. 417 of 479 rows are exact PCO ratios; 62 rows (all in 1951, 1961 and 1972) use the shares of the first later census in which every member has a figure, and say so in weight_basis (proxy_shares_from_1961 / 1972 / 1981). These two tables carry a single population weight (no area weight, since the old district maps are not available as polygons); apply them by merging your data on census_year and historical_district and multiplying by weight. Weights sum to one for every historical district; the allocated totals equal PCO's province totals in 1961, 1972 and 1981 and fall 20,320 short in 1951 (Balochistan; the report's own district rows do not add to its province row that year).

## 3. How the district maps were reconstructed
For each census year a 1998 district either existed (its own unit) or is assigned to the district it was then part of, following the parent chain in the lineage table until a unit that existed. Three districts were formed from two parents and are split at tehsil level using PCO's tehsil figures: Chakwal (Talagang from Attock, the rest from Jhelum), Sanghar (Khipro and Sanghar from Tharparkar; Shahdadpur, Sinjhoro, Tando Adam and Jam Nawaz Ali from Nawabshah) and Kohistan (Dassu from Swat; Pattan and Palas from Hazara). Historical units that are not 1998 districts are named as they were: Hazara (to 1976), Dir (to 1996), Makran (1955-1977), Quetta-Pishin (to 1975), Kachhi (1965-1991, renamed Bolan). Units that were princely states at a census (Kalat, Kharan, Las Bela and Makran in 1951; Swat, Dir and Chitral in 1951 and 1961; Khairpur in 1951; the Bahawalpur State districts in 1951) and Karachi as the Federal Capital Area in 1951 are labelled in the status column; they were enumerated, so their figures are usable, but they were not districts of a province.

## 4. What this does not give, and why
- **No tehsil-level crosswalk from the 1951-1981 maps.** The report restates old counts onto 1998 tehsils; it does not list the tehsils as they stood in 1951 or 1981. Those lists exist only in the District Census Reports of each census, which are not online. The 1998-frame tehsil panel is the substitute: for most of the country it gives every tehsil's 1951-1981 population on its 1998 boundary.
- **Umerkot.** It was a district at the 1998 census (created 1993, abolished December 2000, restored 2004), but the report folds it into Mirpur Khas, so "1998 district" here means the report's frame. A 1998 dataset with Umerkot as a district maps to Mirpur Khas first.
- **Nushki.** The opposite case: Nushki became a district around 2004, after the census, yet the report lists it with restated figures. It is kept as a 1998-frame unit, flagged in the lineage table.
- **Karachi.** East, West and South existed from 1972, Central and Malir from 1996; the report shows one Karachi City District at every census, and Karachi is one unit here as agreed.
- **The Frontier Regions.** Their 1951 and 1961 figures are given by PCO with footnotes, but their creation dates were not found; FR Lakki Marwat and FR Tank are placed inside FR Bannu and FR D.I.Khan before 1972 by name, and flagged.
- **Dates with conflicting sources** are recorded in the lineage notes rather than resolved (Charsadda 1988 or 1998; Pakpattan 1990 or 1991; Musakhel January or November 1992; Makran split 1970 or 1977; Nasirabad's 1974 parent).
- **Pre-1998 within-year dates** (notification months) were only sought for districts, not tehsils.

## 5. Checks
validation_summary_panel.json and validation_summary_backward.json hold the numbers: weights sum to one in every year and in the chained table; the 2023-frame panel reproduces PBS's 2017 and 2023 district rows exactly; totals by year match PCO's province totals except the 1951 Balochistan shortfall noted above.
