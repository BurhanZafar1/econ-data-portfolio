## 1. What the numbers mean
population_mapped_share: the source-year population carried to the target frame divided by the source-frame total (1.000 means every person is placed; the build asserts this for every PBS/PCO layer). split_sources: source units with more than one target. share_rows_w_02_08 and share_rows_w_04_06: the share of rows whose population weight lies in (0.2, 0.8) and (0.4, 0.6), the usual measure of how much of a crosswalk is non-trivial splitting rather than relabelling. share_pop_on_nonexact_rows: the share of the population that moves on rows the resolver inferred (confidence medium or low) rather than rows that equal a published figure or are forced by the published tables; share_pop_on_low_rows: the same for rows with an equally plausible alternative. largest_source_share_uncertain: the largest share, over target units, of a unit's reconstructed source-year population that arrives on inferred rows (the per-unit values are in uncertainty_by_target_unit.csv).

## 3. How to use this as a sensitivity bound
A variable crosswalked with the population weights inherits the inference only through the inferred rows. The crudest bound: for a target unit with uncertain share s, the reconstructed value could be wrong by at most s times the value (if every inferred person was mis-assigned). In the 1998 -> 2023 tehsil layer 81 of 590 target units receive anything on inferred rows, 58 receive a quarter or more and 56 half or more; in the 2017 -> 2023 tehsil layer 28 of 590 target units receive anything on inferred rows, 22 receive a quarter or more and 22 half or more. At district level 35 of 135 target units receive anything on inferred rows, 10 receive a quarter or more and 4 half or more (1998 -> 2023), because almost every inferred flow is between tehsils of the same district and leaves the district total untouched. A sharper check is to rerun an analysis dropping the target units with s above a chosen cut-off (uncertainty_by_target_unit.csv gives s for every unit and layer) and to report both results.

## 5. The uniform-density assumption
Area weights split a new unit's published area between its source units in proportion to people only where a new unit was formed from more than one source (area_basis = uniform_density_split); everywhere else the area weight is the published area of the target unit over the published area of the source (exact). The population weights make no density assumption: they are PBS's own restated counts, or flows chosen to reproduce them. A variable distributed more unevenly than people over the territory of a split unit (for example irrigated land, or a single large plant) will be allocated with larger error than population itself; this is true of every crosswalk built from counts and cannot be fixed without unit-level geography of the variable. Only stock variables (counts) should be crosswalked; rates, shares and means must be rebuilt from their numerator and denominator counts, which the apply functions do when given counts, or averaged with the flow column as the weight.
