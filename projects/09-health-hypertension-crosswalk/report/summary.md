# Correcting self reported high blood pressure: a one page summary

**For:** state and national health officials who use survey answers to track high blood pressure.

## The question

Most surveys ask people whether a doctor has ever told them they have high blood pressure. Planning and burden estimates need the share who actually have it, measured with a cuff or controlled by medicine. How far apart are the two, and can the survey answers be corrected?

## What we did

We used 52,009 US adults in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (1999 to 2018) who both answered the question and had their blood pressure measured. We measured the gap by age and sex, tested several ways of correcting it on survey years the methods had not seen, and applied the best one to every state's telephone survey (BRFSS) results for 2011, 2013, 2015 and 2017, with a range of uncertainty for each figure.

## What we found

* **Young adults over report.** Among adults under 35, 1.7 to 3 times as many say they have been told they have high blood pressure as actually have it when measured (women: 8.4% versus 2.8%).
* **Older adults under report.** At 65 and over, survey answers miss about one case in ten (women: 64% say yes, 71% have it).
* **A tested correction works.** On the three most recent survey rounds, held back for testing, the correction nearly halved the average error, from 4.3 to 2.2 percentage points, and its stated ranges contained the measured value in 29 of 30 groups.
* **States change most at the ends of the age range.** In California in 2017, the corrected share at 65 and over is 64.6% (likely range 60.1% to 68.7%), not the 57.7% reported. For ages 18 to 24 it is 3.1%, not 6.4%.

## What this means for decisions

* Uncorrected survey figures understate the number of older adults who need blood pressure care and overstate it among the young.
* Programmes that size screening and treatment services from surveys should apply an age specific correction, not one factor for everyone.
* The correction should be refreshed as awareness changes: one built on the most recent six years of data was about as accurate as one built on all earlier years but far less biased.

## Cautions

Measured blood pressure comes from one visit, so some people classed as hypertensive would not be on repeat checks. The correction assumes telephone respondents answer like in person respondents of the same age. State results are by age group only, because the published state data do not split age groups by sex.
