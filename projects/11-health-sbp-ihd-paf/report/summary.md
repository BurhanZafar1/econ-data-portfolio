# How much heart disease could blood pressure control prevent? United States, adults 40+, 2018

**For:** a health official deciding how much weight to give blood pressure control in a heart disease strategy.

## The question

Ischemic heart disease (heart attacks and related conditions) killed 365,744 people in the United States in 2018. If no adult had systolic blood pressure above the healthiest level (110 to 115 mmHg), how many of those deaths would not have happened?

## The answer

**About half.** Among adults 40 and older, 51% of heart disease deaths (95% uncertainty interval 47% to 56%) were attributable to blood pressure above that level: about 187,000 deaths (170,000 to 203,000).

| | Share of heart disease deaths due to high blood pressure | Deaths |
|---|---|---|
| Men 40+ | 46% (40% to 51%) | 97,000 |
| Women 40+ | 60% (54% to 65%) | 89,000 |

The share is higher for women because most of their heart disease deaths occur at age 80 or older, where average systolic pressure in women is 147 mmHg.

## How it was worked out

1. Blood pressure measured on 3,499 adults in a national examination survey (NHANES 2017-2018), weighted to represent the US population.
2. How much risk rises per 20 mmHg, from a pooled study of one million adults (Prospective Studies Collaboration, The Lancet, 2002).
3. Official death registrations by age and sex (WHO Mortality Database).
4. The calculation was repeated 1,000 times, varying the survey sample, the risk estimates and the healthy level, to give the ranges shown.

## How far to trust it

* Global Burden of Disease 2019 puts the US figure at 45% (34% to 57%) for all ages in 2019. This result sits inside that range.
* It is a ceiling, not a forecast. Lowering pressure for a few years, as in drug trials, prevents less: with risk reductions seen in trials (for heart disease events) the share is 41% (29% to 53%).
* A single clinic visit overstates how many people have persistently high pressure, so the true share may be a few points lower.
* The survey leaves out nursing home residents, although nearly half of these deaths are at 80 or older.

## What it means

About 79% of the attributable deaths occur in people with systolic pressure of 140 mmHg or more, the level WHO uses to define hypertension, so the larger part of the burden sits among people with hypertension. The other 21% occur between the healthy level and 140 mmHg, which only a downward shift in pressure across the whole population would reach.
