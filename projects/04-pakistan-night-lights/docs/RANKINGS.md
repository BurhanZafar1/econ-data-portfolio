# District rankings by night-time light, 1992 to 2025: are districts stuck?

Built 10 November 2026 by code/07_district_rankings.py; this note is generated by code/08_rankings_report.py from the files in output/rankings, so the numbers below are the files' numbers.

## What was done
Units are the Census 2023 districts of the four provinces plus Islamabad, with Karachi's seven districts counted as one unit: 130 units. Three rankings are built for every year: (i) all 130; (ii) 125 after dropping Islamabad, Lahore, Peshawar, Quetta and Karachi; (iii) 104 after also dropping the 21 districts whose boundary touches one of those five (Abbottabad, Charsadda, Haripur, Harnai, Jamshoro, Kachhi, Kasur, Khyber, Killa Abdullah, Kohat, Lasbela, Mastung, Mohmand, Nankana Sahib, Nowshera, Pishin, Rawalpindi, Sheikhupura, Sibi, Thatta, Ziarat).
Each ranking is computed for three measures: the sum of lights (radiance times area, the size of a district's lit economy), light per square kilometre (intensity), and light per thousand people in the three census years. Rank 1 is the brightest; districts with identical values (in practice, zero light in the early years) share the lowest rank of the tied group. Both source series are used: series B (NPP-VIIRS-like radiance, 1992-2025) as the main series and series A (harmonized DMSP digital numbers, 1992-2024) as the check. The file output/rankings/rankings_district_long.csv holds every rank; ranks_wide_*.csv are the same with one column per year.
Two cautions shape how the results are read. Both series are noisiest around the switch from DMSP to VIIRS inputs (2011-2016), so changes are measured within sensor eras (1992-2010 and 2017 onward) as well as end to end, and climbers are confirmed on three-year window means (1992-94 against 2023-25) rather than single years. And a rank is relative: a district can climb because it grew or because others stalled.

## Headline answer
The ranking is sticky but not frozen. In ranking (iii), the Spearman correlation between the 1992 and 2025 rankings by sum of lights is 0.91 (series B; 0.84 in series A), 58% of districts are in the same quintile in 2025 as in 1992, and the average district moved 9.9 places out of 104. Within the DMSP era (1992 to 2010) the correlation is 0.96 and within the VIIRS era (2017 to 2025) 0.95. Including the big cities changes little (ranking (i): correlation 0.91, 65% same quintile).
Against that stability, a small group of districts moved a long way. Over 1992 to 2025 in ranking (iii), 8 districts climbed 20 or more places and 4 fell 20 or more. Several of the biggest climbers are districts where a large mining, power or port installation is known to have been built in the period; a few are not (section below).

The same names appear whether the end points are single years or three-year windows. Within eras the picture is less uniform: of the eight biggest climbers, Tharparkar, Chagai, Gwadar, Narowal, Mansehra climb in both the DMSP era (1992-2010) and the VIIRS era (2017-2025), while Upper Kohistan (VIIRS era only), Swat (VIIRS era only), Karak (DMSP era only) climb in one era and slip in the other (rank_changes_by_period.csv). Several of the climbers are districts where a large installation is known to have been built or expanded in the period (Thar coal mining and power generation in Tharparkar, the port works at Gwadar, the Dasu dam site in Upper Kohistan, the Saindak mine and the Reko Diq works in Chagai, the Nashpa and Makori oil and gas fields in Karak). That is a reading of the list against general knowledge, not something these data establish, and it is the obvious first hypothesis to test: a single bright installation can move a sparsely populated district a long way in a ranking by total light. Narowal, Mandi Bahauddin, Swat and Mansehra do not fit that pattern and deserve a closer look.

A fall in rank is relative, but in these cases it is not only relative: of the 130 districts, 113 have a larger sum of lights in 2025 than in 1992 in series B and 17 do not (Awaran, Bannu, Barkhan, Charsadda, Hangu, Jacobabad, Kalat, Killa Abdullah, Kurram, Mastung, Naushahro Feroze, Pishin, Sherani, Sohbatpur, Tank, Torghar, Ziarat); 6 of the eight fallers listed are in that second group. Comparing a 1992 level with a 2025 level in series B compares a DMSP-derived value with a VIIRS one, so the within-era changes in rank_changes_by_period.csv are the safer reading.

## Light per person (census years only)
Ranking districts by light per thousand people in 1998 and 2023 (ranking iii, 104 districts): Spearman correlation 0.61. Biggest climbers: Gwadar (70 to 3), Sialkot (74 to 9), Chagai (68 to 11), Tharparkar (75 to 22), Narowal (81 to 30), Upper Kohistan (101 to 53), Karak (80 to 35), Mandi Bahauddin (76 to 32). Biggest fallers: Dadu (22 to 64), Chaman (42 to 88), Loralai (9 to 57), Badin (10 to 66), Nushki (11 to 71). Per-person ranks reward sparsely populated districts with a single large installation, so they should be read next to the size ranking rather than instead of it.

## How much the two satellite series agree
In 2023 the ranking by sum of lights (all units) has a Spearman correlation of 0.93 between series A and series B. Mobility is somewhat larger in series A end to end (correlation 0.84 against 0.91) most likely because series A's VIIRS-converted years carry a faint floor that lifts dim districts (docs/VALIDATION.md, the dim end); within eras the two agree closely. The full table for every series, measure, variant and era is output/rankings/mobility_summary.csv.
